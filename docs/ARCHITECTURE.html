Paper trading research system / architecture v0.4

Thirty-four components, seven layers, one researcher

Every open decision from scoping is now closed. Five independent screens surface candidates from the whole US market by five different mechanisms, the researcher judges each one alone against an absolute bar, and four portfolios run in parallel so the researcher can be measured rather than believed.

SETTLED SINCE v0.3
Long only. Opus 5 as the researcher. $100,000 starting equity at 0.5 percent risk per trade. Postgres. C# throughout with an API layer and a Blazor front end. Five years of backfill before go-live.
THE ARBITRATION RULE
Stated-probability sort with a date-seeded coin flip only where probabilities tie at the cutoff D-18, plus a hard bucket cap in the risk layer. Section 10.
RANK-BASED FILTERING REMOVED
The news ingest previously narrowed to the top 400 names by prior screen score, which made the sentiment screen unable to discover anything it had not already found. Absolute filters now live in the universe definition and nowhere else.
BENCHMARKS CORRECTED
Forward returns are stored three ways. The learning loops read the peer-relative version, because measuring a $500M company against SPY would have caused the tuner to dismantle the two screens that keep this system off megacaps.
PRESENTATION LAYER ADDED
v0.3 had twenty-eight components and no way to look at anything. Layer 7, section 15.

The universe

This is the only place in the system where anything is filtered out. No component downstream narrows by rank, because ranking at ingest decides what can be discovered before anything has had a chance to be discovered.

The mean reversion stabilisation gate

Without it the screen gates on quality and then ranks by whichever surviving name has fallen furthest, which is the falling-knife instinct reintroduced one level below where it was removed. It matters more here than usual because fundamentals are quarterly and keyed on filing date, so the screen's view of a company can be four months stale while the price is current.

ConditionTestPurpose
Price stabilisationclose > 20dma OR rs_20d_slope > 0Buy the turn rather than the fall
News cycle burnoutarticle_count_z_own_90d < 1.0The storm has passed rather than still raging
Sentiment not deterioratingsentiment_delta_7v30 ≥ 0No fresh bad news arriving
The two news conditions fail open. When the 7-day article count is below three the sentiment tests are treated as satisfied rather than failed. Thinly covered names are exactly what this screen's small slots exist to find, and failing closed would delete them and reintroduce the megacap tilt through the back door. The cost is that thinly covered names get a weaker gate than well covered ones, which is deliberate rather than accidental.

Cause classification happens later, not here. A screen computes numbers and cannot read. The researcher receives headlines for mean reversion candidates and its rubric requires it to name the cause of the decline and pass when the cause is structural or unresolved.

S1 and S5 are the megacap risk, and they are correlated. Both are valuation-anchored, and in a drawdown megacaps get cheap against their own history at the same moment they land in the bottom technical quintile. That is a correlated failure arriving exactly when exposure is highest, which is why the quota in section 6 is a hard constraint rather than a tendency, and why the backfill needs a real drawdown in its window.

Megacap bound

Two large slots across five screens is 10 of a possible 40, or 25 percent. Close to the megacap share of total US market capitalisation, so the bound is defensible rather than arbitrary.

Small cap floor

Three small slots across five screens is up to 15 guaranteed places for names between $300M and $2B, subject only to clearing each screen's own floor.

Attribution grain

Around 7,000 rows a year, each tagged with its screen. Roughly 1,400 per screen, which is enough to say something about each within a year.

The attribution write happens here, not later. One row per candidate per night holding that candidate's screen scores exactly as they stood, which screens surfaced it, size bucket, regime and gate state. Forward return columns start empty and C21 fills them as dates arrive. Written at any later point these rows cannot be reconstructed honestly, because by then screen definitions and slot allocations will have moved.
07

What the researcher is given

The model is a judge. It never searches, never computes a ratio, and never sees a raw price series. Everything arrives precomputed.

Prefix tokens and candidate tokens do not cost the same, and the gap decides the design. A token in the shared prefix is written once a night and read twenty-eight times at the cached rate, costing around half a cent a year. A token in a candidate block is fresh input paid twenty-eight separate times at full rate, costing closer to four cents. Candidate tokens are roughly seven times more expensive, so everything shared belongs in the prefix and the candidate block carries numbers and nothing else.

The shared prefix, ~4,600 tokens, cached

Five screen rubrics at roughly 600 tokens each, the field key so candidate blocks can send bare values without labels, active lessons with their sample sizes, the calibration table from the last monthly report, and tonight's market context including regime and sector relative strength. One prefix rather than five, so there is a single cache entry.

The five rubrics

These are where the strategy actually lives. Everything else in the system decides what the model sees. The rubrics decide what it does with it, and without them the model applies generic equity intuition to your data rather than your strategy to your data. They belong in the prefix, where a token costs roughly a seventh of what it costs in the candidate block.

Two weighting principles that apply across all five. Change beats level, everywhere. A company at 12 percent return on capital rising from 8 is more interesting than one flat at 15, because levels favour incumbents permanently while changes are earned. And cause quality dominates magnitude wherever a digest exists: a coverage spike of six standard deviations driven by promotion is worth nothing, while one of two and a half driven by a named contract win is real.

The candidate block, ~800 tokens, fresh per call

A fixed core of around 350 tokens sent for every candidate, then a screen-specific block of around 300 for whichever screen surfaced it. Every metric arrives twice, as the raw value and as its percentile within its size and sector peer group, because the percentile makes it comparable and the raw value gives it magnitude.

Five fields chosen because each can flip a verdict rather than nudge it. Revenue growth trend, because improving margins on shrinking revenue is a different company from improving margins on growing revenue and nothing else distinguishes them. The last two earnings surprises, because for a beaten-down name that is usually the cause and it is the most direct evidence that the quarterly fundamentals are about to turn. Relative strength against its own sector, because down 30 percent while the sector is down 28 is a sector story and down 30 while the sector is flat is a company story. Volume against its 50-day average, which separates a decline on heavy distribution from one on apathy and therefore says whether stabilisation is real. And cash against burn rate for small and mid caps, because a company burning cash will dilute and the share count change field only reports dilution that has already happened.
The harder ceiling was not tokens. The output is BUY or PASS, a conviction integer from one to five, a stop and a target. That is a low-bandwidth decision, and past some point extra evidence moves an internal judgment from 3.4 to 3.6 while the recorded answer stays 3. The coarseness of what is asked for caps the usefulness of what is supplied. If richer input is ever wanted, the thing to widen is the conviction scale, not the block. D-18 The output is BUY or PASS, a stated probability that the trade reaches its target before its stop, a stop and a target. Under the integer scale this note argued that the coarseness of the answer capped the usefulness of the evidence and that widening the scale was the remedy. A percentage removes that ceiling, so the candidate block is the binding constraint again rather than the output. The calibration screen still answers it empirically: a reliability diagram tracking the 45-degree line means the evidence supports the resolution being asked for, and a flat one means it does not. Why ATR is in the core

The model suggests a stop, the risk layer takes the wider of that and twice ATR. A model that cannot see volatility suggests stops that are silently discarded every night.

Why days to earnings is in the core

A 40 percent decline three days before a report is a different proposition from the same decline three weeks after one, and no other field carries that.

The comparability cost

Screen blocks mean candidates do not see identical evidence. It survives because calibration is reported per screen rather than pooled, so the comparison that matters is within a screen where evidence is uniform.

Local enrichment

Article text is where the qualitative gap sits, and full articles run five to eight hundred tokens each. Three per candidate sent straight to Opus would cost around $64 a year and take the total past $137. A local model on the GPU condenses them to roughly 150 tokens first, which costs nothing at the margin, so the year stays near $78.

FieldWrittenWhat it holds
News digestNightly, per candidateWhat happened recently, the apparent cause of any move, and any red flags. Around 150 tokens.
Company one-linerQuarterly, small and mid onlyWhat the business actually does. Closes the gap where the model has no background on a $500M company. Around 30 tokens.
Boundary one: the local model transforms evidence and never judges. It summarises, extracts and classifies. It never scores a candidate, never expresses a view on it, and never filters the candidate set. The moment it does any of those, a research portfolio stops measuring its own model and becomes that model plus a second one, and the comparison against the screens portfolio no longer answers the question it was built to answer.
Boundary two: which model produced each digest is recorded. Swapping the loaded local model, or falling through to the secondary provider, changes the evidence the researchers receive. Without digest_provider and the model name stored alongside every digest, a later shift in results has two explanations and no way to separate them.

The digest provider chain

The digest source is an ordered chain rather than one provider with a fallback bolted on. Each link has a health check, the first healthy one answers, and which one answered is recorded per digest. There is no special-case failure branch, so failover works in both directions without anything in the code knowing which link is meant to be preferred.

OrderProviderWhy here
1Local, LM Studio on the GPUPrimary by choice rather than by cost. Nothing leaves the machine, and an application on your own desk is a stronger bet than a hosted service in public beta.
2Haiku 4.5Deliberately a different provider from the second research portfolio, so a single vendor outage cannot take out both the digest fallback and a portfolio at once. Already integrated for the main researcher, so no new credentials.
The chain is a hard gate on the researcher. If no link is healthy, the run halts before any researcher call is made and no orders are produced for any portfolio. A night without digests is not comparable to a night with them, and skipping a night is free while quietly thinner evidence enters the record looking identical to everything around it.
Two candidates a night always go to the secondary, chosen by the date seed, regardless of whether the primary is healthy. A path that only executes during an outage is broken most of the time and you discover it on the night you needed it. Routing a fixed pair through the second link every night means it cannot rot. It also earns its place twice, because it builds a paired sample where the same nights ran through both providers, so after a quarter you can ask directly whether the digest source changes anything. If it does not, the comparability concern dissolves and either link can be used freely. The rotation costs about $1.30 a year, and a full year of running entirely on the secondary would add roughly $18, taking the total to about $68 and still leaving headroom.

Excluded deliberately. Raw price series, full article text, and business descriptions for large caps since the model already knows them. Article text reaches Opus only as a digest, never in full.

Run the calls promptly. Twenty-nine sequential calls at a few seconds each is two to four minutes and the cache entry lives about five minutes. Run them back to back or in a few concurrent lanes. Spreading them across the evening loses the cache and roughly triples the input bill.

Why a probability rather than a conviction score

The model returns the probability that the trade reaches its target before its stop, as a percentage, in place of an integer from one to five. Three things follow.

The absolute bar acquires a defined value. A trade with a 12 percent target and a 6 percent stop breaks even at a probability of 6 divided by 18, or 33 percent. The rubric can therefore say to buy when the stated probability exceeds the breakeven implied by the reward-to-risk ratio by a set margin, which turns judging against an absolute bar from a disposition into arithmetic. It also forces internal consistency, since the model's own stop and target suggestions now determine the bar its own probability must clear.

Calibration becomes properly scoreable. An integer scale supports little more than checking whether higher numbers win more often. A probability supports a Brier score and a reliability diagram, so the monthly report can state that trades called at 60 percent won 43 percent of the time, which is both a measurement and an instruction.

And ties become rare. A five-point scale over 28 candidates produces ties constantly, which is why the arbitration coin flip was expected to fire often. A percentage has enough resolution that the flip becomes a genuine edge case rather than a routine event.

Change this before go-live, not after. Switching scales later invalidates every accumulated proposal and the calibration history built on it. Nothing has been run yet, so the cost is zero now and rises with every night of operation. Expect the model to cluster at round numbers early on, which is a known weakness and precisely what the calibration loop exists to measure and correct.

The counter-argument field

Twenty words stating the strongest case against the verdict it just gave. It costs around $4 a year and does two things: it measurably improves judgment quality by forcing the model past its first conclusion, and it gives the candidate detail screen the one field worth reading when a decision later looks wrong.

Why the neutral tie-break

Breaking ties by screen rank would import the screens' opinion into the researcher's portfolio, which means the researcher-against-screens comparison is no longer clean. It is also incoherent, since rank 1 in one screen and rank 1 in another are positions in unrelated distributions and comparing them requires normalising onto one scale, which is a composite ranker rebuilt at the last step. Breaking ties by thinnest size bucket imports a diversity preference instead, which is better but still puts a consistent thumb on the scale.

The coin flip carries no opinion, which is what makes the rule measurable later. Every rejected BUY is logged with its stated probability, so after a year you can compare what the flip took against what it skipped. Those two groups differ by nothing but chance, so any gap is real. Under either alternative the taken group would be systematically different and the comparison would measure the bias rather than the rule.

Two things that are easy to confuse. The bucket cap, at most four of eight open positions in large-and-above, is a risk constraint and applies to all four portfolios. The coin flip applies to the two research portfolios only. And nothing is divided between the portfolios: all candidates go to all four in full, and they can hold the same stock at the same time.

The portfolios

Four at launch: Research: Opus 5, Research: V4 Pro, Screens and Random. All run off the same candidate set with the same gates, sizing, stops, slippage and starting equity. Only the selection function differs.

ComparisonAnswers
Research vs ScreensWhether the researcher adds judgment the screens do not already contain. This is the question the project exists to answer.
Research vs ResearchWhether the judgment has to come from a frontier model. Every variable except the model is held identical, including the dossier, the rubrics, the arbitration steps and the random seed. If the cheaper one keeps pace, this task is rubric-following rather than hard reasoning and the annual cost drops to single digits with evidence behind it.
Screens vs RandomWhether the ranking inside each screen carries information, or whether the membership test does all the work and the ordering is noise.
Random vs SPYWhether the filters and screens together beat plain market exposure. If they do not, nothing downstream matters. SPY is a reference line, not a portfolio.
Exposure matching applies to Screens and Random only. They do not judge, so without matching they would stay permanently invested and outperform in any rising market for reasons that have nothing to do with selection. Both follow the entry count of whichever research portfolio is designated primary. A research portfolio abstains on its own terms, so the two research portfolios are never matched to each other, and the difference in how often each stands down is itself part of what the comparison measures.

The research portfolios are configuration, not code

Screens and Random are fixed. They have no model and nothing to swap, so making them configurable would be machinery with no purpose. The modularity is entirely on the research side, because that is where you will eventually want to turn one off, or point it at a model that does not exist yet.

ColumnHolds

Everything downstream keys on portfolio id: proposals, orders, fills, positions, trade outcomes and the cost ledger. The PortfolioRunner iterates the registry rather than assuming a fixed set, and the UI charts whatever is active, so adding a third research portfolio or dropping to one changes nothing but a row.

Only the research portfolios are candidates for retirement on performance. They are competitors, judging the same candidates by different means, so the one that loses over a long enough run has answered its question and can go. Screens and Random are controls and must never be cut for underperforming, because their underperformance is the measurement. If Random were to beat Research after a year, retiring it would be deleting the most important result the system had produced.
Abstention is measured from attribution, not from a portfolio. Exposure matching means the primary research portfolio's decision to stand down is never tested by Screens or Random, and it does not need to be. Every candidate on a full-pass night still has an attribution row with forward returns, so you can ask directly whether the nights A stood down were worth standing down.

Why the learning loops cannot read the SPY column. The universe runs to $300M and the quotas guarantee up to fifteen of forty slots to small caps. Measured against a large-cap index in a large-led market, every small-cap candidate posts negative alpha regardless of how well it was chosen. The sentiment and flow screens are the two that structurally tilt small and the two doing the most to keep this system off megacaps, and the tuner would cut both to the floor of four slots while the trend screen collected the same effect as a reward. It would spend a year quietly dismantling the anti-megacap protection the whole design was built around, and every number on its monthly report would look defensible. The peer benchmark needs no ETF, since the percentile engine already groups the universe into cells and daily prices exist for every name in them.
Why slot reallocation rather than weight tuning. Under a single composite the tuner could only report that a weight moved from 0.40 to 0.43, which is neither interpretable nor actionable. Per screen it reports that the sentiment sleeve ran a 41 percent hit rate over 90 observations while the flow sleeve ran 58 over 80, and the action follows. The floor of 4 slots stops a screen being switched off before it has produced enough observations to judge, and the cap of 12 stops the system collapsing back into one screen.

Three rules the filler needs from day one

Acquisitions need an explicit rule, because a candidate bought out three weeks in has no 63-day return in the ordinary sense and dropping those rows deletes a chunk of your winners. Delistings and bankruptcies need the same treatment in the other direction, or survivorship bias enters your own attribution table. And rows never complete until 63 trading days have passed, so every analysis carries a three month blind spot at the recent end.

Stack and runtime

C# THROUGHOUT

.NET across ingest, compute, select, decide, execute and learn. The data provider maintains Python and Node clients but not C#, so a thin typed HTTP client is written here. Doing it directly is what keeps the point-in-time discipline under your control rather than inside someone else's model binding.

POSTGRES

Chosen for concurrent writers during backfill rebuilds rather than for size. Twelve million screen score rows recompute every time a screen definition changes during development, and Npgsql binary COPY turns that from a long insert loop into a short job. The nightly run would have been fine on SQLite.

API AND UI

ASP.NET Core minimal API, read-only, as the single surface the Blazor client talks to. Keeping it read-only means no UI action can ever alter a run, and the pipeline stays the only writer.

Backfill

Five years before go-live, not two. The screens' floors need 250 days of history before they mean anything, and the tuner and calibration report need populated attribution to work from month one rather than month six. The deeper reason for five is that the window must contain a real drawdown, because that is the only condition under which the quality and mean reversion screens converge on the same megacaps, and you want to see that happen in history rather than discover it live.

Density is correct, and it only works if every number carries its own interpretation. A trading terminal can be dense because price and volume explain themselves. Almost nothing here does: peer-relative alpha, slot fill, floor, Brier, an article count z-scored against a ticker's own baseline are all inventions of this project, and a bare figure for any of them is unreadable. The answer is not help text, which adds clutter to screens that are already full. Five rules instead. One. No number appears without its comparison and its sample size. Not "alpha 2.4%" but "+2.4% vs peers, n=340". The comparison is what makes it mean something and the count is what says whether to believe it. Two. Every threshold the system already knows is drawn rather than remembered. The screen floor, the position caps, the breakeven implied by a stop and target, the pre-registered threshold. If the line is on the chart nobody has to recall its value. Three. Every screen opens with one sentence stating what it currently says, generated from the data rather than written. "Both models agreed on 24 of 27 tonight and passed on all of them." "The sentiment screen has filled under half its slots for three weeks." The table below is the evidence for that sentence. Four. Definitions arrive on demand from GLOSSARY.md rather than being written into the interface, so a term cannot drift from the document that defines it. Five. Insufficient data is shown as insufficient. For the first months most of these views cannot support a conclusion, and the honest display is the observation count against what is needed, not a chart that looks finished.
The visual hierarchy has to fight the reading order, and the interface should help rather than hinder. Equity curves are the most compelling thing here and the least informative for the first two years, because each portfolio produces only 125 to 250 closed trades a year against the roughly 400 per arm needed to separate them. The attribution table carries around 7,000 observations a year and answers the primary question in one. So the primary claim view is first in the navigation and the portfolios view carries its own trade count and an underpowered marker, rather than leaving a reader to remember a caveat written in another document [VALIDITY.md §3].
The local model status is persistent chrome, not a panel on one screen. A digest silently missing because the inference server was not running is the most likely operational failure in this system, and it is invisible in the output: the run completes, the proposals look normal, and the evidence was simply thinner that night. So the chain state sits in the application frame on every screen, showing which link is currently answering rather than a bare up or down, and a readiness check runs shortly before the evening window rather than at the moment of use, so a failure is reported while there is still time to start the local server. Running on the secondary is a visible state rather than a silent one. The run health screen holds the detail and the reconnect control.
One deliberate exception to the read-only API. Connecting to and configuring the local model is the only write the UI can make, and it is scoped to local_model_config alone. No endpoint can touch run data, so the property that matters is preserved: nothing a user does in the interface can alter a completed run or the record of one. The exception is worth naming because it is the seam where that guarantee would erode if more writes were added later.

Parallelism

The pipeline has two natural partition keys and they are not interchangeable. Getting this wrong is the difference between a backfill that finishes in minutes and one that finishes overnight, and it is also the way subtle correctness bugs get in.

StagePartition keyHow
Historical price and fundamentals ingestTickerEmbarrassingly parallel. Bounded Parallel.ForEachAsync, each worker writing through its own Npgsql binary COPY stream.
Indicator computationTickerEvery indicator is a function of one ticker's own series, so no worker needs to see another's data.
Valuation computationTickerSame. Price and that ticker's own filings.
Percentile computationDateCannot partition by ticker. A percentile on a given day needs every name in the cell on that day. Expressed as a single window function with PARTITION BY size_bucket, sector, so Postgres parallelises it and no application-side threading is involved.
Screen scoringDateSame reason and same shape. Set-based, one statement per screen.
Screen floorsSecond passSee below. The 250-day trailing dependency is removed rather than parallelised.
Researcher callsCandidateBounded to three or four lanes, deliberately not more. Rate limits apply and the prefix cache lives about five minutes, so the goal is finishing inside that window rather than maximum throughput.
Learning loops, including calibrationNoneNot parallelised. See the note below.
The two-pass backfill. Each screen's floor is the 98th percentile of its own trailing 250-day score distribution, which reads like a sequential dependency where day D cannot be computed until days D-250 through D-1 exist. It is not. Pass one computes raw screen scores for every ticker on every date with no dependency on any other date, fully date-parallel. Pass two computes floors and applies them in a single window function over the now-complete score table. A dependency that would have forced 1,260 sequential steps becomes a parallel job plus one query.
Calibration does not need parallelism, and adding it would be a mistake. The calibration report is an aggregation over the attribution table, bucketing stated probability against realised peer-relative return, grouped by screen [D-18]. That is roughly 7,000 rows a year and about 35,000 after the full backfill. It is one GROUP BY and it finishes in well under a second. The same is true of the screen tuner and the lesson writer, which run monthly over the same table. Parallelising any of them would add concurrency bugs to a job that is already instant. The stages that genuinely need it are the backfill and the nightly compute layer, and those are specified above.
What stays strictly sequential. The stages themselves. Ingest completes before compute begins, compute before select, select before decide, decide before execute. Parallelism lives inside a stage and never across stages, because the freshness guard and the zero-row halt both depend on a stage being finished before the next one reads it. A partial run is worse than no run.

Where convergence kept trying to reappear

The single risk this design exists to manage is that everything quietly collapses onto the same hundred well-known large caps. It surfaced in four different places during scoping, each time in a component that looked unrelated to selection. Worth recording, because it will try again.
